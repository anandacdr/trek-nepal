What a rest day in Namche actually looks like

It is not a day off. It is the day that decides whether the rest of your trek works.

Published 21 April 2026

It is not a day off

Day three of an Everest trek is marked "acclimatisation day, Namche Bazaar" and almost everyone reads that as a lie-in. It is not. You will walk up several hundred metres, spend some time there, and come back down to sleep — climb high, sleep low, the principle the whole itinerary is built on.

Your body reacts to the altitude you sleep at, not the altitude you touch. So the day's work is done up at the Everest View Hotel or Khumjung, and the recovery happens back down in Namche at 3,440 m.

What the day looks like

Up at seven, because the mountains are clear in the morning and cloud in from about eleven. Breakfast, then a slow two hours up the hill behind the town.

At the top: Everest, Lhotse and Ama Dablam across the valley, a cup of tea at 3,880 m, and — for most people — the first genuine sense of the scale of what they have walked into. Then back down for lunch, an afternoon in the town, and an early night.

Namche is a working town, not a viewpoint. There is a Saturday market that has been running for a very long time, bakeries that are much better than they have any right to be, a couple of decent gear shops, and the last reliable ATM on the trail.

The mistake people make

Do not skip it

Every season, trekkers on a tight schedule try to buy back a day by pushing straight through Namche. It is the single most common reason a trek ends early. The days you feel you are wasting at 3,440 m are the reason the days at 5,000 m work.

The second mistake is treating the rest day as a rest day and lying in bed. Complete inactivity is not what acclimatisation wants — a gentle climb and a return is measurably better. Your guide is not being cruel.

Practical notes

  • Cash. Draw what you need in Namche. There is no reliable ATM higher, and lodges above here are cash-only.
  • Charging. Cheapest here it will be for a week. Fill every battery.
  • Showers. Same. Above Dingboche a hot shower becomes expensive and, frankly, a bad idea in the cold.
  • Gear. Anything you forgot can be bought or hired here, at a Khumbu markup. Better than carrying it from Kathmandu and better than going without.
  • Wi-fi. Works. Expect it to stop being useful somewhere above Dingboche.
